Service Dog Treat Dispenser

Our team built a treat dispenser for a real client: a man who uses a wheelchair and works with a service dog. The goal was to let him reward his dog on his own from his chair. I designed the treat magazine, the part that stores the treats and feeds them to the dispensing end.

  • Designed the magazine as a long, clear acrylic channel built from tab-and-slot side, top, and bottom panels, so the treat level is visible at a glance.
  • Designed a rubber-band gate at the outlet: two bands stretched across acrylic brackets hold the stack of treats in place, then flex to let one treat through when it's pushed out. That makes getting a treat out easy for him from his chair, with no latch or door to work. A 3D-printed PLA cap closes the other end.
  • Modeled every part in CAD and made the assembly drawing and bill of materials.

Cam-Driven Embosser

The assignment was to take a mechanism that converts rotary motion into linear motion and build a working device around it. My partner and I built a motorized embosser. My part was the mechanism: the drivetrain, the cam press, and how the dies load into and out of the machine.

  • Designed a 3:1 gear reduction with laser-cut gears to drive the cam.
  • Designed the cam and the guided die holder it lifts, which presses the die up into its negative in the top case.
  • Designed a layered top case so the dies swap in and out without taking the machine apart, with magnets holding them in place.
  • Modeled the full assembly in CAD and checked the final assembly drawing.

Crusader Helmet

Our group was asked to use the manufacturing processes from class to build something of our choosing. We built a crusader-style helmet from aluminum sheet, working from a published paper pattern and turning it into waterjet-ready CAD. I did most of the CAD and helped manufacture the panels.

  • Built paper and cardboard prototypes from a printed pattern to check the shape before cutting metal.
  • Redrew the pattern by hand in SolidWorks and scaled it from a real head measurement.
  • Added rivet tabs to the CAD after a scrap-aluminum test piece showed the panels wouldn't line up without them.
  • Cut the panels on the waterjet, formed the back on a table bender, and drilled mating panels together to keep the rivet holes aligned.

Truss Analysis & Load Test

My partner and I designed and built a Warren truss, predicted how it would carry load, and then tested those predictions on the physical truss.

  • Calculated support reactions and member forces by hand for a center load and an off-center load.
  • Modeled the 7-joint, 11-member truss in analysis software to check the hand calculations and find joint displacements.
  • Found how moving the load shifts the reactions, from an even split at center to roughly 5:1 when the load is off-center.
  • Built the physical truss and loaded it to compare real behavior against the calculations.

Wind Tunnel Design & Build

I led a team that designed and built a working 17-foot wind tunnel over one school year, from the first layout sketches through fabrication and a running tunnel.

  • Led the airflow optimization work, including a flow straightener made from a bundle of paper tubes, achieving laminar flow through the test section.
  • Designed the whole tunnel to be easily disassembled into its separate sections.
  • Planned and coordinated fabrication across the team, and we finished on schedule.
  • Ran smoke flow-visualization tests on a sphere mounted in the test section, viewed through the clear acrylic side panel.
